---
name: Flashcard learning boundaries
description: Why self-rated recall is separate from scored learning and shares the free vocabulary allowance.
---
Treat self-rated flashcard recall as study, not evidence of quiz accuracy or word mastery; do not award quiz XP or mastered-word credit from “Easy.”
**Why:** Learners choose their own ratings without demonstrating a correct answer. Counting these as mastery would make learning statistics and ranks misleading.
**How to apply:** Keep recall ratings and scored quiz outcomes separate. A scheduling connection is allowed, but self-ratings never manufacture quiz answers, streaks, XP or mastery.

Flashcards and Words should be partially connected, not identical practice modes.
**Why:** The user agreed to a connection where wrong quiz answers bring words into flashcards sooner, repeated correct quiz answers reduce repetition in both, and “Forgot” can bring a word back into a later quiz. “Easy” changes flashcard spacing only; both modes must retain some new words.
**How to apply:** Use the other mode as a bounded scheduling hint, not shared completion. Newer evidence should supersede an older hint; avoid an old mistake permanently overriding a subsequent successful review. Preserve existing histories rather than inventing missing historical recency.

Flashcards share the existing free vocabulary round rather than creating an additional unlimited practice route.
**Why:** The user previously explicitly closed a free vocabulary replay loophole and limited free vocabulary to one five-minute daily round; adding a new presentation should not undo that decision.
**How to apply:** Preserve the shared daily allowance unless the user explicitly changes free-plan access.

Newly shown flashcards must be immediately flippable, including during their entrance animation.
**Why:** The user objected to having to wait after a card appeared before revealing its meaning.
**How to apply:** Keep the flip and entrance animations, but do not use their completion as a reveal prerequisite. The user also wants visibly animated progress growth; that feedback must stay decorative, respect reduced motion, and never delay card interaction. Retain persistence and duplicate-rating guards separately.
